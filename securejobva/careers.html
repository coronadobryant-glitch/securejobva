Before you apply

What the online job needs from you.

These online jobs hire on capability, not location. But a seat runs on live calls and shared screens, so the setup below is not negotiable.

You

The person, not the hardware.

  • 18 or older, with the legal right to work as an independent contractor where you live
  • Fluent English, spoken and written — clients hear you and read you every day
  • Government photo ID, and a background or police check where your country issues one
  • A fixed shift you can hold, which may fall in the evening or overnight for you
  • A quiet, private place to work — no cafes, no shared rooms during calls
  • Prior experience in your track helps, but a strong assessment beats a long CV

Your setup

Checked on the technical interview.

  • Intel i3 (10th gen or newer) or AMD Ryzen 3 and up
  • 8 GB RAM minimum, 60 GB free storage
  • Windows 10/11 or macOS 12 and newer
  • Wired broadband, 10 Mbps down / 5 Mbps up or better — a mobile hotspot cannot be your main connection
  • Noise-canceling headset and an HD webcam
  • A backup plan for power and internet, because your shift still starts

Close but not exact? Apply anyway and tell us what you have. We will tell you what needs upgrading before you spend anything on it.

Why the bar is where it is

Seven things businesses have been burned by.

Every client who calls us has been let down by someone in an online job before, and it is almost always one of these seven. The exams, the strengths test, the fixed hours and the background check all exist to answer them — which is why clearing them is what makes the seat worth paying for.

01

Communication gaps

Time-zone mismatches, accents that are hard to follow, replies that take hours, and problems the assistant saw coming but never flagged.

What we ask of you: Hold your shift in American time, write and speak clearly, and raise a problem the day you spot it — not the week it lands.

02

Quality control and oversight

No way to verify the work is actually happening, no clear record of output or time spent. What starts as a question becomes distrust.

What we ask of you: Work on tracked time your client can audit, and end each day with what you handled and what still needs them.

03

Vetting and skill mismatch

A résumé and an interview say one thing; the first real task says another, and the client is left with someone who cannot do the job.

What we ask of you: This is what the written task and the qualification exams are for. Pass them on your own ability and the mismatch never reaches a client.

04

Training overhead

Clients underestimate the weeks it takes to onboard someone, especially on niche tasks — time they were trying to buy back in the first place.

What we ask of you: You are trained on our payroll, on the client’s tools and scripts, before your first shift. You arrive ready, not learning.

05

Turnover and reliability

Assistants who ghost, quit without notice, or hop between clients — forcing the business to re-hire and re-train from zero.

What we ask of you: One business, one manager, a fixed seat. If you need to leave, you give notice and hand over properly.

06

Security and confidentiality

Handing a remote worker access to financial accounts, customer records and passwords feels reckless without a contract and real safeguards.

What we ask of you: You sign a contract and an NDA, clear an ID and background check, and follow the password and data rules from day one.

07

Payment and platform friction

Currency conversion, international transfer fees, escrow disputes, and billing nobody can read — hourly one week, per task the next — turning pay into an argument.

What we ask of you: Keep your timesheet honest and submit it on the weekly cycle. The client is invoiced for hours worked, so your hours are the invoice — raise anything that looks wrong before the run, not after it.

Questions

Straight answers.

Does it cost anything to apply?

No. Not to apply, not to be trained, not to be placed. Anyone asking a virtual assistant for a fee to be hired is running a scam — that includes anyone claiming to be us.

Which countries are these online jobs open to?

Any country where you can legally work as an independent contractor and cover a full shift in American time. What decides it is your English, your connection and your overlap — not your passport.

What hours does the online job run?

Forty hours a week on fixed hours in American time — Eastern, Central, Mountain or Pacific, depending on the client. That means your shift may be evening or overnight where you are. You see the exact hours before you accept, and they do not move without your agreement.

Is the training really paid?

Yes — once you pass the exams and are approved. Those two steps come first and they are unpaid, like any interview. Clear them both and the training that follows is paid work, starting within one week of your approval.

What is the strengths test?

A short assessment of how you actually work — where you are quick, where you are careful, and how you hold up under pressure. We use it to place you in the track and the seat you will excel in, rather than the first one with a vacancy. There is no pass mark on it and no wrong answer.

Do I need previous online job experience?

Not formally. Most of our placements come from call centers, retail, admin and support roles. The assessment carries more weight than the CV.

Am I employed by you or by the client?

You are contracted through us and managed by us. The client directs the day-to-day work; we handle your schedule, reviews, pay and any escalation.

What happens if the client stops?

You go back into the pool with our reference and we prioritize you for the next matching seat. You are not left to restart from an empty profile.
