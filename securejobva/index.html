The seven things that go wrong

Every way this usually fails, and what we do about it.

Most people who call us have hired a virtual assistant before and it did not work. These are the seven reasons why, and the specific thing on our side that answers each one — not a promise to try harder.

01

Communication gaps

Time-zone mismatches, language and accent barriers, replies that take hours, and assistants who never raise a problem until it lands on you.

How we solve it: Your seat works your hours in your time zone, English is tested in a written task and two live interviews before you meet anyone, and you get a summary at the end of every day — problems flagged the day they appear, not the week after.

02

Quality control and oversight

No way to verify the work is actually happening, and no clear record of output or time spent. What starts as a question turns into distrust.

How we solve it: Time tracking you can audit yourself, so the hours you are billed are the hours you can see, plus a daily written account of what was handled and what still needs you.

03

Vetting and skill mismatch

Résumés and interviews do not reveal real competence. The mismatch shows up once the work starts, and by then it is your problem.

How we solve it: Nobody reaches you without clearing a written task in their track, the qualification exams and a strengths test. Then the free first week shows you real work before you commit.

04

Training overhead

Onboarding takes far longer than expected, especially on niche tasks — and that time was exactly what you were trying to buy back.

How we solve it: Training happens on our payroll, not your calendar. Your assistant is onboarded on your tools and your scripts before the first shift, so day one is work, not setup.

05

Turnover and reliability

Assistants who ghost, quit without notice, or job-hop between clients — forcing you to re-hire and re-train from zero each time.

How we solve it: One business, one manager, one fixed seat — no gig queue to hop to. Sick days and holidays get backup coverage, and if the fit stops working we replace the seat free and hand over for you.

06

Security and confidentiality

Handing a remote worker access to financial accounts, customer records and passwords feels reckless without contracts or safeguards in place.

How we solve it: Every assistant signs a contract and an NDA and clears an ID and background check before they start, and works to written password and data rules from the first shift.

07

Payment and platform friction

Currency conversion, international payment fees, escrow disputes, and billing that is never quite clear — hourly, task-based, or something in between.

How we solve it: One flat rate, $7.75 an hour worked, invoiced weekly in US dollars. No escrow, no conversion, no platform cut, and no task-based pricing to argue about — you pay the number you were quoted on the first call.

Questions

The things people actually ask.

Will my VA work my hours?

Yes. Your seat is scheduled to your time zone — a standard business day, an evening block, or split coverage across the morning and night. You set the shift on the scoping call and can change it with a week of notice.

Is this shared or dedicated?

Dedicated. One person, working only for you, in your tools and your systems. Not rotating between five other clients, and not a pooled ticket queue.

What if the person is not working out?

Tell your account manager. We start a replacement search the same day and you are not billed for the overlap. No replacement fee, and no minimum you have to serve out first.

How do you handle passwords and customer data?

Every VA signs an NDA and a data-handling agreement before day one. We recommend a shared password manager rather than sending credentials directly, and we set access to the least level the role needs. You can revoke all of it yourself at any time.

Do I need my processes documented already?

No, and almost nobody does. Documenting them is part of onboarding week — we sit with you, write the SOPs from how you already work, and hand you the document at the end whether you stay with us or not.

Is the first week really free?

Yes — you are not billed for the hours your assistant works in week one. We do set up a card and a deposit before the week starts, so the seat can begin the day you approve it rather than waiting on paperwork. Billing itself starts in week two, at $7.75 an hour worked.

What is the minimum commitment?

None. Billing is weekly and you can end it with two weeks of notice. We would rather earn the next week than lock you into a year.
